Bars show ground roll & 50-ft distance against runway available. Published figures assume a perfect short-field technique — always add a personal margin.

For planning and training only — not a substitute for the approved AFM/POH. Base figures are interpolated from Figure 5-4 (Takeoff Distance, Short Field) of the Cessna R172K POH and assume its stated conditions: flaps 10°, 2600 RPM full throttle, mixture at placard fuel flow, cowl flap open, paved/level/dry runway, and the short-field technique of Section 4. The POH badge marks the manufacturer's own wind (Note 2) and dry-grass (Note 3) corrections. The ADVISORY badge marks wet/contaminated-surface and slope adjustments, which are not in this POH — they are conservative planning rules of thumb drawn from FAA / Flight Safety Foundation guidance and may differ from your conditions.

On takeoff, the dominant wet-runway hazard is not the ground roll itself but the inability to stop if you reject, plus aquaplaning in standing water. Real performance is further affected by gross weight, CG, engine and propeller condition, grass length, surface firmness, obstacles, and pilot technique. Verify every departure against current data and your own minima. The pilot in command is solely responsible for the safe conduct of the flight.
